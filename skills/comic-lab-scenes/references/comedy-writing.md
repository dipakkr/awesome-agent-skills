# Comedy that teaches

Use this as a flexible rhythm, not a requirement that every joke use identical wording:

1. **Confident mistake (0–4s):** a specific action the character has already taken. “I put my laptop on the roof” is more playable than “What is cloud storage?”
2. **Visible consequence (4–9s):** rain on the laptop, envelopes erupting, a parcel growing. The joke should read in a still image.
3. **Deadpan correction:** short, concrete, no canned laugh track. A silent hold of 0.3–0.6s can make a response land.
4. **Useful explanation:** the learner asks the next sensible question. One diagram at a time. Remove jargon before adding jokes.
5. **Boundary:** a real caveat or practical next step. Do not sacrifice accuracy for the analogy.
6. **Callback:** reuse the original object with a changed interpretation. End while the joke is still fresh; hold the takeaway afterwards.

Keep the learner likable and curious. Their overconfidence drives the joke, not their identity, accent, appearance or intelligence. A tutor can be dry without being cruel. Avoid generic “plot twist” lines and repeated “actually…” corrections. Test whether the final line is still funny without explaining it.

The supplied examples demonstrate different targets:

- **Literal interpretation:** remote storage becomes literal weather.
- **Missing reward:** clicking “accept cookies” never produces food.
- **Escalating machine:** a useful automation multiplies a bad rule.
- **Unearned confidence:** an AI awards itself a diploma before its outputs are meaningfully tested.
- **Visual exaggeration:** an allegedly tiny request grows beyond its box.

For marketing, substitute a verified product mechanism for the explanation, and a relevant next step for the takeaway. Keep the comedic scenario clearly fictional. Do not insert invented testimonials, fake metrics or a promise that the product guarantees results.

Before rendering, read both roles aloud. Cut any sentence that repeats the board. Give the tutor the shortest punchline. Leave at least one genuinely useful idea a viewer can repeat afterwards.
